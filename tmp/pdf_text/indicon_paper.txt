Does Channel-Wise Decay Improve Associative
Recall in Small Gated DeltaNets?
Mayank Mehta, Michelle Aloz, Amrita Pati, and Sujata Deshmukh
Department of Computer Engineering
Fr. Conceicao Rodrigues College of Engineering
Mumbai, India
Abstract—Channel-wise decay gives each key coordinate in
a Gated DeltaNet its own forgetting factor. We compare this
design with scalar decay under matched training conditions on
synthetic recall and overwrite tasks, and extend three published
ﬁxed-state test cells with matched scalar models. Fresh baseline
accuracy was near ceiling, while the ranking changed with the
task and metric under stress. In the constrained-state cell, scalar
decay had 1.68 percentage points higher mean accuracy and
both variants reached lock-in in all ten runs. Channel-wise decay
had an exploratory advantage on latest-value retrieval across
overwrite stress conditions. A ﬁxed-state diagnostic with ﬁller
extending recall examples to 1,024 tokens exposed large seed-
speciﬁc failures in both gate types. Across the tested tasks and
training budgets, channel-wise decay did not yield a consistent
recall advantage.
Index Terms—Gated DeltaNet, associative recall, linear atten-
tion, recurrent memory, decay granularity, synthetic benchmarks
I. INTRODUCTION
Efﬁcient sequence models often replace the growing key–
value cache of attention with a ﬁxed-size recurrent state. Gated
DeltaNet combines a data-dependent forgetting factor with a
delta-rule update, which corrects the stored association when
a key is observed again [1]. Implementations differ in the
granularity of the decay: the original GDN uses scalar decay
per head, while Kimi Delta Attention uses channel-wise decay
[2]. Greater granularity is a plausible way to allocate different
retention timescales within one head, and recent large-scale
architectures use it [3].
Whether this ﬂexibility improves associative recall in small
matched models is a distinct empirical question. Large hybrid
models differ in many factors besides decay granularity, while
associative-recall benchmarks can be sensitive to model state
size, training recipe, and data layout. Prior controlled work has
already compared decay designs in language modeling, so a
scalar-versus-channel-wise comparison alone is not a novelty
claim [4]. This study instead reports a controlled, small-model
comparison alongside a published-harness reproduction and
measurements of training time and model footprint.
The paper asks: Does channel-wise decay provide a reli-
able recall advantage over scalar decay in the small Gated
DeltaNets tested here? The contributions are:
• A matched comparison of scalar and channel-wise decay
on small synthetic recall and overwrite tasks, including
held-out stress cells and decay interventions.
• A reproduction and scalar-versus-channel-wise extension
of three cells from a published ﬁxed-state associative-
recall harness, with ten seeds per variant.
We also report model footprint and short sequential optimizer-
step measurements.
II. BACKGROUND AND RELATED WORK
A. Fixed-state sequence models
The Transformer stores context explicitly through attention
[5]. Linear attention can instead accumulate feature products
in a recurrent matrix whose dimensions are ﬁxed with respect
to sequence length [6]. This representation connects to fast
weight programmers, which use one stream of activations to
write weights that another stream reads [7]. A delta write
corrects the state prediction for the current key. For an exact
repeated key, that correction directly addresses the difference
between the current read and the desired value. The same
update can affect related keys when they occupy overlapping
directions.
Fixed-state alternatives differ in their update and retention
mechanisms. RetNet uses retention with head-dependent de-
cay [8]. Mamba uses input-dependent state space parameters
[9], and state space duality explains how some state space
computations can be expressed through structured attention
operators [10]. Gated linear attention and parallel delta-rule
algorithms provide related routes to efﬁcient computation [11],
[12]. Mamba-2 restricts the diagonal transition used in Mamba
to a scalar times the identity within each head. GLA applies a
vector gate to the key coordinates of the state. These separate
architectures illustrate the two design choices. Our comparison
varies decay within a single delta-rule family, with the state
shape and correction rule held ﬁxed.
B. Gated recall and repeated writes
GDN augments a recurrent fast-weight memory with for-
getting and delta-rule correction [1]. Kimi Linear introduces
channel-wise decay through Kimi Delta Attention, and Gated
DeltaNet-2 further separates erase and write control [2], [3].
RWKV-7 combines vector-valued decay with a generalized
delta update [13]. This is a precedent for the combination, with
an update and parameterization distinct from the controlled
channel-wise GDN arm.
Associative-recall evaluations include Multi-Query Asso-
ciative Recall (MQAR), introduced in the Zoology study of

efﬁcient language models [14]. Recent work emphasizes the
roles of state constraints, interference, and training curricula
in ﬁxed-state recall [15]. We ﬁrst reproduce the published
channel-wise arm and then add scalar decay in the same
harness.
The closest controlled study compares multiple decay and
write mechanisms across model sizes and language-model
datasets [4]. Accordingly, this paper makes no priority claim
for comparing scalar and channel-wise decay. We exam-
ine how gate granularity affects recall across synthetic task
conditions in small models. Kernelized linear attention also
evaluates recall and repeated-key overwrites under constrained
state capacity [16]. That setting motivates distinguishing the
latest value for a written key from the continued availability of
unrelated values. An aggregate overwrite score can combine
those outcomes even when they respond differently to load or
training.

B. Experiments
Paired scalar and channel-wise models were assessed on
MQAR and overwrite stress cells across three seeds and
three initialization conditions. Channel interventions on trained
channel-wise checkpoints tested whether performance de-
pended on channel differences; these are checkpoint interven-
tions, not independently trained static-gate baselines.
We also reproduced three cells from the public “Anatomy of
Associative Recall” harness before running scalar models with
matched seeds and commands. The cells were constrained-
state recall (E1), distractor haystack (E4), and collision keys
(E5), each with ten seeds. Reproduction checks were frozen
against the published channel-wise results. Outcomes include
mean accuracy and the cell-speciﬁc lock-in count; the metrics
are not interchangeable.
C. Synthetic tasks and training controls
Figure 2 summarizes the evaluation. MQAR presents key–
value pairs followed by queries about previously seen keys.
The base cell uses sequences of length 64 and four asso-
ciations. The overwrite task presents a key with an initial
value and a later replacement, then queries both the updated
association and an unrelated one. We score exact answers at
query positions and examine unrelated retention separately.
For answer-free overwrite evaluation, earlier gold answers are
erased from the input. This isolates one source of leakage
while preserving the speciﬁed query layout.
The paired runs share data order and the initialization of
common parameters within each seed. The 16-epoch default
and calibrated matrix uses seeds 42–44 and 3,136 completed
optimizer updates per checkpoint. Training uses 100,000 ex-
amples, FP32, and AdamW [17], with batches of up to 512
examples accumulated in microbatches of up to 32. The initial
learning rate is 10−8/3 (approximately 2.15×10−3). It follows
an epoch-level cosine schedule with a 64-epoch horizon, zero
ﬂoor, and no warmup. Weight decay is 0.1, with the decay
rates and offsets exempt. A nonﬁnite loss or gradient aborts
the run. The update count records actual AdamW calls. The
ﬁxed endpoint gives both gate types the same update budget.
For the fresh endpoint check, each task and seed uses 512
examples with the same realization across the paired models.
Exact duplicates with regenerated training, development, and
previous conﬁrmation examples were excluded by the overlap
audit.
D. Initialization conditions
Default initialization retains the recipe’s sampled timescales.
The mean-decay condition ﬁts the offset of each gate using
training activations so its empirical mean decay equals 2−1/h,
with successive block targets h ∈{16, 32, 64, 128}. These tar-
gets describe isolated constant-decay factors. Memory survival
in the full model also depends on later writes, interference,
and readout. Matching decay at initialization reduces a source
of variation when comparing granularity, and the ﬁtted pa-
rameters remain trainable. The joint condition ﬁts offset and
rate to target mean log decay and mean Softplus sensitivity.

E. External replication
The three Anatomy cells probe different conditions. E1
uses a constrained matrix state, E4 adds distractors to long
retrieval, and E5 stresses recall when keys collide. The scalar
extension runs with the corresponding seeds 0–9 and cell
settings. E1 has 32 associations, key width 32, one layer,
and 6,000 optimizer steps. E4 and E5 have sequence length
64, four pairs, two layers, two heads, and 3,000 steps. E5
includes a rebinding control. The original harness arm uses
a separate decay value per key coordinate in the executable.
We deﬁne the comparison by that implementation and keep
the cell conﬁgurations distinct from our synthetic recipe. We
report mean accuracy for each cell and lock-in counts where
relevant. Lock-in requires ﬁnal accuracy of at least 0.90 for
E1 and 0.80 for E4 and E5. This count complements mean
accuracy, which is inﬂuenced by the size of both successes
and failures.

IV. RESULTS
A. Synthetic recall and overwrite
Table I shows fresh endpoint accuracy for the default and
mean-decay conditions. MQAR is close to ceiling for both
gate types. The joint runs enter the separate stress suite.
The pooled latest-value accuracy was 99.95% across the 12
overwrite checkpoints. Most residual errors concerned the
unrelated association.
Scalar decay has higher mean overwrite and retention accu-
racy under both initializations in Table I. The direction varies
within seed pairs. Under mean-decay initialization, scalar leads
by 6.54 points on seed 42 and channel-wise leads by 3.71
points on seed 43.
The overwrite stress result measures a different endpoint.
Table I scores both query types on fresh examples with four
associations, one rewrite, and the nominal eight-token query
gap. The separate stress-suite summary averages latest-value
accuracy across nine conditions: six baseline and shift cells
(base task, increased load, longer query distance, and two
to four rewrites) and three paired update diagnostics. It also
includes joint initialization. Channel-wise decay led in eight
of nine pairs by a mean of 3.17 percentage points on that
endpoint. Restricting this comparison to the six baseline and
shift cells gives eight of nine pairs and 3.39 points. Scoring
both queries across the full nine-condition suite changes the
count to ﬁve of nine. The exploratory latest-value result
therefore cannot be read as a general improvement in overwrite
and retention. The paired points in Fig. 3 show the effect of the
task, seed, and initialization. On MQAR, channel-wise decay
led in four of nine pairs in the six-cell mean, with the positive
average driven by a single low-performing scalar run.

E. Measured runtime and resource footprint
Table V summarizes the available resource measurements.
The current four-layer recipe contains 1,585,416 trainable
parameters with scalar decay and 1,651,456 with channel-wise
decay, a 66,040-parameter (4.17%) increase. At FP32 weight
storage this is approximately 6.34 MB versus 6.61 MB. Both
variants use the same recurrent-state shape: four value-by-
key matrices of size 256 × 128 per sequence, or 512 KiB in
total at FP32. Thus, channel-wise decay increases learned gate
parameters but does not increase the persistent recurrent-state
dimensions in this comparison.

F. Overwrite layout diagnostic
In a separate follow-up on one custom overwrite generator,
all six control models (three per gate type) met the shortcut
criterion on a fresh test. The models often returned an obsolete
value when all writes to the queried key appeared before
unrelated writes. When training varied both query distance and
rewrite count, all six retrained models cleared the criteria for
the shortcut and for sensitivity to query distance on a second
fresh test. This observation applies to the generator and layouts
tested.
V. DISCUSSION
Across these experiments, gate granularity alone did not
predict recall reliability. In E1, the near-ceiling accuracy dif-
ference left the lock-in outcome unchanged. Across overwrite
stress conditions, channel-wise decay had an exploratory edge
on latest-value accuracy. The E4 mean-accuracy difference
and task-dependent results rule out a blanket claim that scalar
decay dominates. The training-seed counts and task coverage
limit the precision of these contrasts.
A. Threats to validity
The principal limitations are the small model size, short
training budgets, and use of synthetic tasks. The ﬁller-
extension sweep stresses elapsed retention at ﬁxed load. It
leaves open whether channel-speciﬁc timescales help when
a long sequence contains both new writes and old associa-
tions that must remain accessible. The exploratory overwrite
comparison has uncorrected multiplicity and partially shared
data across paired models. Finally, checkpoint gate averaging
measures reliance of already-trained models on gate diversity,
not the value of training a new static-channel architecture.
B. Quality and resource trade-off
For L single-head blocks with residual width dmodel and key
width dk, widening the decay projection adds Ldmodel(dk−1)
weights. The learned rates and offsets add 2L(dk −1) pa-
rameters. Both widths equal 128 in our recipe, giving 66,040
additional parameters over four blocks at the same recurrent
state size.
A shared decay also simpliﬁes the transition algebra. In
the structured state space duality (SSD) formulation used by
Mamba-2, the scalar-identity transition gives one decaying
factor per head for each pair of positions. This permits a
block decomposition that uses dense matrix multiplications
within chunks and passes recurrent state across chunks [10].
GLA’s vector gate gives coordinate-speciﬁc decay products.
Its implementation uses secondary tiling within chunks to
recover tensor-core work for part of that computation [11].
These are implementation examples from distinct recurrences.
Scalar decay also simpliﬁes the decay component in GDN,
whose delta correction still has to be computed [1], [12]. This
algebra motivates testing simpler scalar kernels in workloads
where recall is comparable. The brief A6000 measurements
do not establish an optimized kernel speed ranking.
VI. CONCLUSION
In the small Gated DeltaNets and tasks evaluated, channel-
wise decay did not provide a consistent associative-recall
advantage over scalar decay. A reproduced published harness
showed scalar-favoring constrained-state accuracy, a lock-in tie
in the haystack cell, and an uncertain collision result. Channel-
wise decay increased the parameter count while leaving the
recurrent-state size ﬁxed. The shared decay factor may sim-
plify kernel design. The ﬁxed-state length extension reveals
large failures in both gate types, with the direction of the
mean contrast reversing across initialization conditions. The
experiments leave open the effect of training under mixed short
and long retention demands.
