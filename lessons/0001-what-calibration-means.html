Lesson 1 · Your gating experiment

Choose the starting forgetting behavior.

That is what calibration means here. Before answer-prediction training starts, we adjust existing gate offsets so their average decay on training inputs reaches a chosen target. This is initialization calibration, not probability-confidence calibration.

1. First understand the multiplier

The model stores memory as numbers. On each token, its gate multiplies the previous memory by alpha, then the delta rule updates it. A factor of 0.95 keeps 95% in this decay operation; 0.99 keeps 99%. Small differences compound.

Scalar gating applies one multiplier across a head's memory state. Our channel-wise variant has 128 controls per layer, one per key-coordinate column. These coordinates are learned numbers, not named slots for individual facts.

2. The actual change we make

  1. Create the model with its normal seeded initialization.
  2. Take the first 256 training examples, without reading labels.
  3. Turn dropout off and observe the gate inputs.
  4. Adjust only each existing dt_bias offset until its mean alpha reaches the layer target. Earlier layers are fitted first.
  5. Restore training mode and RNG state, then train all parameters normally.

The same layer target applies to scalar and every channel. We are matching their initial averages, not forcing identical gate functions. After training starts, the gates may move away from those targets.

Actual seed42 scalar offsets were approximately −3.4121, −6.0972, −6.2659 and −5.6897. Notice that these are not 16, 32, 64 and 128. The model's other rate and projection parameters determine which offsets produce the requested mean.

The half-life is derived from constant decay: alphah = 1/2. Our real alpha changes with inputs, and memory also receives writes. A target of 16 does not promise “the model remembers half the answers after 16 tokens.”

3. Did our calibration work?

As a fitting operation, yes. Both seeds reached all requested mean decays with maximum errors below 0.0000001. Only the offsets changed during fitting; model capacity did not increase. The implementation and numerical checks passed.

As a way to improve learning, not consistently. The following is the retention learning-curve score over epochs1–16, expressed on a 0–100 scale. It measures how well unrelated associations were preserved during learning, not only at the endpoint.

Calibration helped scalar in seed42 but hurt it in seed43. It slowed channel-wise retention learning in both. Therefore a correctly fitted initial target is not automatically a better starting point for learning. Two seeds do not yet explain the mechanism or establish a universal ranking.
